# Skill-Level Memory — Fermi Estimation (Reference Implementation)

**Author**: Teacher reference — do not distribute to students before capstone

---

## Lessons Learned

### Lesson 1: Anchoring matters more than chain precision
**Situation**: During pilot testing, groups with a strong anchor (a real, verifiable number)
produced estimates within 3x of actuals. Groups who started with a vague "about..." anchor
were off by 50x–200x.  
**Why it matters**: The first number in the chain dominates. If the anchor is bad, no
amount of careful multiplication fixes it.  
**Implication for the model**: Step 1 should emphasize "verifiable" not just "known."
Ask students to name their source for the anchor number.

### Lesson 2: Students confuse "estimate" with "guess"
**Situation**: Multiple groups wrote "I don't know" for the chain steps instead of
making explicit assumptions.  
**Why it matters**: A Fermi estimation requires committing to numbers, even uncertain ones.
The discipline of writing down "I assume X because Y" is the point.  
**Implication for the model**: The prompt should explicitly say "write down your assumption
for each step, even if you're making it up. You can flag uncertainty — you cannot skip."

### Lesson 3: The sanity check is where real learning happens
**Situation**: A group estimated "10 million Americans affected by X" and then checked
against the US population (330M) — their estimate was 3% of the population, which
felt plausible. But when they checked "how many new cases of X per year" against CDC data,
they realized their anchor was off by 10x.  
**Why it matters**: The sanity check step is where students encounter their own blind spots.
This is also where the skill connects to epistemics — knowing when you don't know.  
**Implication for the model**: Lesson 5 (Second-Order Thinking) is a great follow-on.

## Known Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Trigger | Detection | Workaround |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I'll just Google it" | Any question with a findable answer | Student skips the estimation step | Require the estimation BEFORE looking it up; compare |
| Precision theater | Student does the chain with 4 significant figures | Chain has ×1.37 × 0.94 × 12.2... | Remind: order of magnitude only. Round aggressively. |
| Scope creep | "How many people..." turns into "How many people who also..." | Chain has 6+ steps | Cap at 4 steps; force simplification |

---

## When NOT to Use Fermi Estimation

- When the user has a precise answer available and time to find it (this model is for when they don't)
- When the decision doesn't depend on scale at all (some decisions are binary regardless of size)
- When the user is already committed — Fermi is a pre-commitment tool. Post-commitment, use Pre-Mortem instead.
