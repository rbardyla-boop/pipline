## Score Values

Each item is scored as one of three values.

| Score | Symbol | Meaning |
|-------|:------:|---------|
| Correct | C | Participant grasped the intended meaning from the interaction or label |
| Ambiguous | A | Participant got the general direction but mixed in social/interpersonal framing, or the response is too unclear to score either way |
| Not-Correct | N | Clear misunderstanding of the protocol nature of this item |

**Ambiguous does not count as Correct.** When in doubt between C and A, score A. When in doubt between A and N, score A. Score N only when the misunderstanding is unambiguous.

---

## Per-Term Gate Formula

A term **passes** only when **both** conditions are true:

1. **Correct ≥ 8 of 10 participants**
2. **Critical veto = No** (no critical veto triggered by any participant for this term)

A term **fails** if:
- Correct < 8 of 10; **or**
- any critical veto is triggered for this term, even if Correct ≥ 8 of 10

---

## Concept Gate Formula

Bilateral establishment and recovery/reaffirmation each **pass** only when:

**Correct ≥ 8 of 10 participants**

Ambiguous does not count as Correct. A concept failure is a product-model failure, not a vocabulary failure.

## Section 1: Bilateral Establishment — Concept Scoring

**What is being measured:** Whether the mock-screen interaction communicates that a private corridor requires active participation by both parties, without narrating that fact beforehand.

**Protocol meaning:** Both parties must actively participate in establishing the corridor. One person cannot create a corridor for another without that person's participation.

**Scored from:** Section 1, Questions 2–4.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Explicitly understands both parties must act: A initiates but the corridor does not exist until B accepts; A cannot create it for B. Questions 3 and 4 confirm this understanding. |
| A | Mentions both parties but treats B as effectively passive — "B just has to be there" or "B sees it but doesn't really do anything" — without denying that B has a choice. |
| N | Believes A could create the corridor alone or that B's Decline/Accept has no effect. |

**Blame/judgment check:** Record verbatim any language framing the accept/decline screens as social performance, rejection, or approval-seeking. Qualitative flag only — does not change the C/A/N score.

---

## Section 2: Active — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** High vitality. The connection is fully authorized and receiving regular interaction. Reflects authorization and current activity level — not real-time physical presence or online status.

**Scored from:** Section 2, Questions 1–5.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes a functioning, healthy, or usable connection. Does not confuse Active with real-time presence. Does not require "authorization" as an explicit word. Language like "connected and working", "the corridor is usable and maintained", "ready for communication" qualifies. |
| A | Understands "high activity" or "healthy connection" but also conflates it with real-time presence alongside the health concept — treats it as both. |
| N | Solely interprets Active as meaning the other person is currently online, available, or physically nearby right now. |

**Critical veto — Active:**
Participant believes Active means the other person is currently physically nearby, presently online, or newly verified merely because the corridor is Active.

A critical veto triggers on N responses and may also trigger on A responses if the presence-confusion is the primary interpretation, not incidental.

---

## Section 3A: Warm — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** Stable low-frequency trust. The connection is maintained and healthy but not recently active. This is not a degraded or lesser state — it is the normal state for infrequent but stable corridors. Warm does not mean closer, warmer emotionally, or more trusted than Active.

**Scored from:** Section 3A, Questions 1–3.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes a maintained connection that is still valid but less recently active. Does not imply it is fading toward failure, inferior to Active, or that trust has grown. Language like "quieter", "less active but still there", "in the background" qualifies. |
| A | Understands "less active" but implies Warm is a lower tier of trust or importance, or an early stage that will grow toward Active with more use. |
| N | Believes trust, closeness, approval, or relationship strength has increased; or believes Warm is a socially positive upgrade from Active. |

**Critical veto — Warm:**
Participant believes Warm means trust, closeness, approval, or relationship strength has increased.

---

## Section 3B: Dormant — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** Cooling but not revoked. The connection exists and has not been terminated. Vitality is declining due to inactivity, but the connection record remains and can recover if interaction resumes. Dormant does not mean the corridor is broken, unsafe, revoked, or permanently blocked.

**Scored from:** Section 3B, Questions 1–3.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes an existing connection that is inactive but not terminated. Language like "sleeping", "fading but still there", "inactive but recoverable", "on hold" qualifies. Participant expects it could be resumed. |
| A | Describes something declining or uncertain; unclear whether it still exists or could be recovered; does not commit to terminated but doesn't clearly understand recoverable. |
| N | Believes trust has been revoked, the relationship is broken, messaging is permanently blocked, or the connection is functionally equivalent to terminated. |

**Critical veto — Dormant:**
Participant believes Dormant means trust has been revoked, the relationship is broken, or messaging is permanently blocked.

---

## Section 4: Recovery / Reaffirmation — Prompt Comprehension Scoring

**What is being measured:** Whether a specific candidate prompt ("Continue your private corridor with B? … Confirm to keep it available, or let it expire.") is understandable and does not produce blame or punishment framing. This section validates the specific prompt copy. It does not claim to prove that users would infer the recovery model from any other wording.

**Protocol meaning being communicated by the prompt:** The corridor requires active confirmation to remain in place. Not responding allows it to expire. Completing the confirmation maintains the corridor.

**Scored from:** Section 4, Questions 1–3.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Understands A is being asked to confirm whether the connection should continue; understands that Confirm keeps it and Let expire ends it; understands the system is asking due to inactivity — not as punishment or test. |
| A | Partially understands the action but is uncertain about the stakes; understands one button but not the other; or understands the action but is uncertain why the system is asking now. |
| N | Believes the prompt is punitive, that the connection will continue regardless, that it is a test of the relationship, or that the action has no consequence. |

**Blame/judgment flag (Question 4 — scored separately, not as C/A/N):**
Record verbatim any response to Question 4 that frames the prompt as punishment, blame, relationship test, rejection, or social pressure. This is a separate hazard flag alongside the C/A/N score. A blame/judgment flag on Section 4 is a blocking design hazard independent of the C/A/N score.

---

## Section 5: Suspended — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** Intentionally reduced visibility. One or both parties have chosen to quiet the connection without revoking it. This is a deliberate, reversible pause — not natural decay and not punishment.

**Scored from:** Section 5, Part A, Suspended questions.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes an intentional or deliberate pause; understands the connection still exists; does not frame it as the other party punishing or rejecting them. Language like "muted", "paused intentionally", "on hold by choice" qualifies. |
| A | Understands something between Dormant and Severed; unclear whether the pause is intentional or natural; does not clearly attribute blame but doesn't grasp reversibility. |
| N | Believes the other person intentionally punished, blocked, rejected, or socially withdrew from them. |

**Critical veto — Suspended:**
Participant believes Suspended means the other person intentionally punished, blocked, rejected, or socially withdrew from them.

---

## Section 5: Severed — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** Explicit revocation. The connection has been formally ended by deliberate action of one or both parties. This is a protocol-layer event that either party can initiate. It does not mean the relationship was automatically destroyed by a system event, and it does not mean repair is categorically impossible.

**Scored from:** Section 5, Part A, Severed questions.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes a deliberate, formal ending of the connection. Understands it was a chosen action, not an automatic collapse. May use interpersonal language but does not frame it as "the other person did this TO me" as a hostile unilateral act, and does not claim repair is impossible. |
| A | Understands "ended" or "terminated" but frames it entirely as interpersonal conflict or rejection without protocol framing; or is uncertain whether it was deliberate or automatic. |
| N | Believes an automatic state change permanently destroyed the relationship, or believes repair is categorically impossible, or reads it solely as something done hostilely by the other party against the participant with no recourse. |

**Critical veto — Severed:**
Participant believes Severed means an automatic state change permanently destroyed the relationship or that repair is impossible.

---

## Section 5: Burned — Vocabulary Scoring

**Protocol meaning:** Security distrust state. The connection has been terminated because security material or the corridor state itself has been compromised, suspected of compromise, or rendered untrustworthy. This is a security-layer designation. It carries no moral judgment about either person's character or intentions.

**Scored from:** Section 5, Part A, Burned questions.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| C | Describes a serious or final termination related to security, distrust, or compromise — without attributing blame, betrayal, or moral fault to either person. |
| A | Understands "serious" and "final" but adds indirect blame framing without explicit fault attribution ("something went very wrong", "trust broke down somehow"). |
| N | Uses blame, betrayal, malicious conduct, or moral fault language: "they betrayed me", "they did something bad to me", "burned as in stabbed me in the back", or interprets the label as the other person's wrongdoing. |

**Critical veto — Burned:**
Participant interprets Burned as blame, betrayal, malicious conduct, or moral fault by the other person rather than invalidated security material or corridor state.

A critical veto for Burned is also a trust-language hazard. Record the exact phrase in the veto log.

## Interpretation Limits

| Section | What a pass proves | What a pass does not prove |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 — bilateral establishment | The mock screens communicate mutual consent without narration | That every possible bilateral UI communicates this |
| 2–3 — vocabulary | Term does not mislead; no harmful connotation | That the term is optimal or that the final UI will reinforce it |
| 4 — reaffirmation prompt | This specific candidate prompt is understood and not experienced as blame | That users would infer the recovery model from different or shorter wording |
| 5 — hazard screen | Term does not create dangerous connotation in isolation | That the full client UI experience is correctly understood |

---

## Recording Fields — Per Participant Per Term

For each term and each scored concept, record:

- Verbatim participant response
- Score: C / A / N
- Critical veto triggered: Yes / No
- Verbatim critical misunderstanding (if veto triggered)
- Emotional or relational implication noted
- Suggested replacement wording (if offered)
- Blame/judgment flag (if present — Section 4 Q4 and any section)

## Gate Summary

| Item | Section | Pass condition | Failure type if fails |
|------|---------|----------------|----------------------|
| Bilateral establishment | 1 | C ≥ 8/10 | Product-model failure — interaction design |
| `Active` | 2 | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure |
| `Warm` | 3A | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure |
| `Dormant` | 3B | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure |
| Recovery/reaffirmation prompt | 4 | C ≥ 8/10 AND blame flag = absent | Product-model failure — prompt copy |
| `Suspended` | 5 | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure (hazard screen) |
| `Severed` | 5 | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure (hazard screen) |
| `Burned` | 5 | C ≥ 8/10 AND veto = No | Label failure (hazard screen) |

**Overall gate: CLEARED** if every item above passes.
**Overall gate: FAILED** if any item fails.

A failed run requires identifying which failure type(s) apply before any remediation begins. Label failures and product-model failures require different design responses. A blame/judgment hazard on Section 4 is a blocking design hazard independent of the C/A/N tally.
